# Preserves

Fictional demo data. Thistledown Kitchen is an invented cookbook and every recipe below is made up.

## Setting point

Jam sets at 105 C. Without a thermometer, spoon a little onto a cold saucer, wait 30 seconds, and push it with a finger: a set will wrinkle.

Under-set jam can be boiled again the next day. Over-set jam cannot be rescued, so test early and often.

## Quince and bay jelly

Makes about 4 jars. Chop 2 kg of unpeeled quince and cover with water. Simmer for 90 minutes with 3 bay leaves, then strain through a cloth overnight without pressing it.

Measure the juice and add 450 g of sugar per 600 ml. Boil hard to setting point, which usually takes 15 minutes.

## Salted damsons

Fills one 1 litre jar. Layer 800 g of damsons with 80 g of salt and 2 teaspoons of coriander seed. Seal and leave at room temperature for 3 weeks, turning the jar daily for the first week.

They keep for a year in the fridge once opened.

## Jar hygiene

Wash jars in hot soapy water and dry them in a 130 C oven for 15 minutes. Fill them while both jar and preserve are hot, and seal at once. A lid that does not dip in the middle once cool has not sealed, so keep that jar in the fridge and eat it first.
